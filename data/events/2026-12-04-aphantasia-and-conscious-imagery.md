## Abstract

Around 4% of people report congenital aphantasia: they cannot voluntarily visualize objects, yet they can describe their visual appearance and accurately solve tasks thought to involve mental imagery. What neural mechanisms produce this cognitive competency despite the absence of subjective imagery? Aphantasia offers a unique window onto the mechanisms that make internally generated representations conscious.

In this talk, I will present results from ultra-high-field 7T fMRI, diffusion tractography and eye-tracking that characterize the behavior and neural circuits underlying visual mental imagery, in typical imagers and in individuals with congenital aphantasia. Building on these findings, I will outline a three-stage model which describes how internally generated representations give rise to conscious imagery: imagery generation, integration, and amplification. I will also demonstrate how the model could be tested using the new paradigm.
